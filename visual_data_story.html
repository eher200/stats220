I specified that the studying must be done at a desk and at home, because otherwise it could become subjective (for example, would sitting in a lecture theater count as studying at a desk?). In terms of data collection, I started by observing the number of minutes to the nearest 10 minutes. Next, I wanted to measure what the most frequently studied paper was, so in the question I asked what paper I spent the most time on during this period of time. I often study more than one paper when studying so that’s why I specifically asked for the paper I spent the most time on. I also asked what mode of learning I used primarily - paper or digital. I hardly ever use paper so most of the data showed that I learned digitally. I added another question to the observational logging form, where if the responded responded with "digital" as the main learning mode, then I would ask whether it was "iPad" or "Laptop". This is reflected in the second graph on this visual data story. Altogether, this provided me with information about how long I study, what I study and how I study. ## What days do I have the most study sessions? I decided to analyse the days of the week that I studied, and the total number of individual study sessions I have for each. *According to the data, I tend to study the most on X.* This likely reflects when assignments are due. I study less on the weekend as I like to try and relax a bit. This graph is meaningful because I can use it to plan my week, by working out approximately how much work I should do on each day. ![A vertical column chart comparing the number of study sessions per day of the week, with Thursday having the highest number of study sessions](plot3.png) ## What percentage of my study sessions are primarily laptop-based? I decided to compare what percentage my laptop was used while studying (compared to iPad or paper). *According to the graph, I use my laptop the most compared to non-laptop strategies.* This is meaningful because it is helping me work out how I study best so I can apply it in my next courses. ![A bar graph comparing the percentage of study sessions using a laptop (67%) vs not using a laptop (33%). The bars are pink and the background is yellow with an orange border](plot1.png) ## Do I study STATS or PSYCH papers for a longer time on average, when studying for longer periods of time? I decided to compare STATS papers with PSYCH papers, to work out which I tend to study more, specifically when I am studying for extended periods of time (which is why I selected the 20 longest study sessions). *On average, I tend to have longer study sessions when studying STATS.* This is meaningful because I can now set alarms before I start studying STATS so I remember to take regular breaks. It also means I shouldn't study it when I am short on time, otherwise I might get distracted and spend too much time doing it. ![A dot plot comparing the number of minutes studied in PSYCH papers compared to STATS papers, out of the 20 longest study sessions. Stats is studied for longer with an average of almost 100 minutes](plot2.png) #### Further Analysis of this graph I think this could be for 2 reasons: 1. STATS 220 is harder than all of my other psych papers, so I have to stick at things for longer. This is expecially because my observational logging occurs in periods where the project is due (and projects require more work compared to no-project weeks) 2. I am biased, and remember that I have to do observational logging when doing STATS 220, and forget when studying psychology papers Realistically, I think it's definitely a mix of both! ## Final takeaway I tend to study STATS more when studying for extended periods of time, and I also tend to study on a laptop more, as and tend to study the most on Thursdays and Fridays. This will help inform my future study habits as I know how I can create a more balanced and varied study schedule now, by identifying the parts that I tend to put lots of emphasis on. ## Meme time ![A humourous GIF comprised of 3 rotating graph frames with text above reading "my dreams after working on project 4:" to illustrate how it takes up this person's internal thought processes](project4_meme.gif)
